# YouTube Video Sharing Database – Problem Understanding

## 1. Project Overview
The objective is to rebuild a reliable and well-constrained PostgreSQL database for YouTube's video-sharing platform. The database must support video uploads, channels, subscriptions, watch events, reactions, comments, playlists and advertisements. It should also support reporting and future growth.

## 2. Business Problems and Stakeholders

| ID | Business Need and Affected Stakeholder | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Channel pages, video watch pages and playlists must load quickly for viewers. | Customer-facing / Real-time |
| P2 | Subscription feeds and watch history must respond quickly for active users. | Customer-facing / Real-time |
| P3 | Search users need fast video title searches. | Customer-facing / Real-time |
| P4 | Creators and YouTube Studio need timely daily views, traffic source, retention, likes and subscriber growth reports. | Reporting / Analytics |
| P5 | Creators need an accurate list of subscribers who have never watched their channel. | Reporting / Analytics |
| P6 | Recommendations need efficient Shorts shelf and Trending tab calculations for viewers. | Customer-facing / Real-time |
| P7 | Publishing teams need reliable scheduled publishing, fast uploads and efficient view-counter updates. | Customer-facing / Real-time |
| P8 | Finance and Partner Program teams need reliable ad revenue, monetization eligibility and Premium ad audit reports. | Reporting / Analytics |
| P9 | Trust and Safety teams need child-safety, copyright and moderation reports. Growth teams need logged-out viewing and watch-time trends to plan for 20× traffic growth. | Reporting / Analytics |

## 3. Assumptions
1. Each channel belongs to one registered user.
2. A video has an 11-character ID and can be a long video or a Short.
3. Video visibility can be PUBLIC, UNLISTED, PRIVATE or SCHEDULED.
4. Watch events can be generated by signed-in and logged-out viewers.
5. Only signed-in users can subscribe, react, comment and create playlists.
6. YouTube Premium members should never receive advertisements.
7. Database relationships and constraints must preserve data integrity.
8. Performance improvements must not change the results of the given 30 SQL queries.
9. The supplied CSV exports represent the available source data; no undocumented business rules are assumed.

## 4. Performance Priorities
Customer-facing and real-time operations should prioritize low response time and reliable writes. Reporting and analytics operations should prioritize accurate results and efficient aggregation over large datasets. The database design should also consider future traffic growth of up to 20×.
